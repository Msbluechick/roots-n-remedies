What we build

Five pieces of software. What each one is, who it is for, and what it will not do.

My Damn Self

Your evidence, organized into one packet you can hand to somebody.

You already have the proof — it is just scattered across two years of screenshots, text threads, photos, voice memos, letters and PDFs. My Damn Self takes all of it and gives back one packet: everything in date order, every item numbered, the text inside pictures read and searchable, recordings transcribed, and tables that lay out what happened, when, and which pieces back each other up. For a lawyer, an officer, a caseworker, or a judge.

Your files are read on your own device. They are not uploaded, and nobody here ever sees them. It organizes and counts — it does not decide what anything means.

mydamnself.com

Due Process Audit

The rule, the record, and the arithmetic between them.

Put a published rule — a statute, a court rule, an agency policy — beside what a record actually shows, and Due Process Audit does the math nobody has time to do: what the rule required, what date it was due, what the record says happened, and where the two do not meet. It shows its work and cites the rule it used, so anybody reading it can check it.

It reports. It does not rule, it does not argue, and it is not legal advice.

dueprocessaudit.com

Roots to Roux

Know before you forage.

Point your camera at a plant and get an identification — with the safety questions asked first and the dangerous look-alikes shown right beside the match, not buried three screens down. Built for the yard, the ditch and the woods behind the house, around what actually grows where you are, along with what it has traditionally been used for.

It never tells you something is safe to eat. It tells you what it looks like, what it could be confused with, and what to check.

ProCierge

Your actual town, on your phone.

The businesses, the events, the services, the help that exists three streets over — on a map of the place you really live, kept by verified people instead of anonymous accounts. Made for the towns that never get their own app and are told to make do with a Facebook group.

In testing now. It goes out when it is right, not when it is ready enough.

My Damn Mirror

Your own record, read back to you.

The same careful reading the other tools do, turned around and pointed at your own patterns — what you keep saying, what you keep walking back into, what changed and when. Quiet, private, and yours.

This one is not evidence. It does not build packets, it does not go to court, and nothing in it is for anybody but you.
